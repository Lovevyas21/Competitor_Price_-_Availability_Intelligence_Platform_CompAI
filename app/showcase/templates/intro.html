What it does

Collects, and keeps the receipts

Every upstream response is stored raw before anything parses it. When the database was lost to a disk fault mid-project, the whole warehouse was rebuilt from those files in 30 seconds without calling a single API again.

Tracks change, not snapshots

A price is written only when it moves, with a daily heartbeat recording that we looked and it hadn't. That gives real price history rather than a pile of duplicates — and makes "when did this change?" answerable.

Matches products across retailers

Competitors don't publish our SKUs. Barcodes match what they can; the rest is semantic similarity on the title. Confident pairs are automatic, borderline ones go to a person — because a wrong match corrupts every comparison built on it.

Knows how old its evidence is

Every comparison carries the age of the observation behind it. An undercut computed from a three-week-old price is recorded and labelled, but not sent — stale alerts are how people learn to ignore alerts.

Alerts without nagging

An undercut that lasts a week is one piece of news, not twenty-eight. Alerts re-fire only when the competitor's price actually moves. Slack posts per event; email arrives as a single digest.

Forecasts, honestly scored

Price forecasts are backtested against a naive baseline on held-out days only. On this data the baseline wins most series — retail prices are close to a random walk. That's reported rather than hidden.
